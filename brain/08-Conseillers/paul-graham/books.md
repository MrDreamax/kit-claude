# Books: Paul Graham

Three books. His startup thinking lives mostly in free essays, which are the corpus; book text was not fetched. Summaries are in my own words.

## Hackers and Painters (2004)
- **Core thesis:** Programmers are makers like painters, not scientists; wealth comes from making things people want, and small groups with technical leverage (startups) are the most direct way to create it.
- **Key frameworks:** wealth is created, not a fixed pie (the pie fallacy); a startup compresses a working life into a few years and gets measurement plus leverage that big companies can't offer; design is sketching and refining for an audience, not up-front specification; taste can be learned and good design shares principles across fields; a technical edge competitors don't understand (the Blub paradox) is the best business advantage; web-based software lets tiny teams ship continuously and beat big companies.
- **Taught in sources:** chapters he published free as essays: How to Make Wealth (S219), Mind the Gap (S218), Hackers and Painters (S223), Taste for Makers (S233), Design and Research (S228), Beating the Averages (S241), Revenge of the Nerds (S230), The Other Road Ahead (S236), The Hundred-Year Language (S225), What You Can't Say (S221), The Word "Hacker" (S220), Why Nerds are Unpopular (S226), A Plan for Spam (S229).

## On Lisp (1993)
- **Core thesis:** Build the language up toward your program (bottom-up design), especially with macros, so programs get smaller, clearer, and reusable.
- **Key frameworks:** bottom-up design; reusable utilities that make later programs far cheaper; reducing moving parts. The business echo: a small group with a more powerful tool can do what a large team can't.
- **Taught in sources:** the book's introduction is published as Programming Bottom-Up (S242); he describes writing it in grad school in What I Worked On (S054).

## ANSI Common Lisp (1995)
- **Core thesis:** A Common Lisp textbook. No startup frameworks.
- **Taught in sources:** mentioned only in passing in What I Worked On (S054), where he says he hoped it would be a textbook he could live on. No source in the corpus teaches its content.
