# Warren Buffett: books

Buffett never wrote a conventional book. His "books" are the shareholder letters and the Owner's Manual, which are in the corpus in full and public on berkshirehathaway.com. Below them are the books he teaches from in public. Summaries are in this brain's words; no book text is reproduced.

## His own writing

### Berkshire Hathaway chairman's letters (1977 to 2024)
- **Core thesis:** Run a company as a partnership for owners: measure per-share intrinsic value, allocate capital rationally, avoid debt and ruin, keep managers autonomous, and report candidly.
- **Key frameworks:** owner earnings, look-through earnings, float and its cost, economic goodwill, the institutional imperative, the dollar-retained test, the acquisition criteria, great/good/gruesome businesses, Noah's Law.
- **Sources:** S050 to S097 (48 letters).

### An Owner's Manual (1983 principles, booklet issued 1996, updated since)
- **Core thesis:** The owner-related business principles: partnership attitude, eating your own cooking, per-share intrinsic value as the goal, sparing debt, no issuance below value, never selling good businesses, and candid reporting.
- **Key frameworks:** the 13 owner-related principles and later additions; the five-year retained-earnings test; intrinsic value versus book value.
- **Sources:** S098.

### "Berkshire: Past, Present and Future" (2014 letter, fiftieth anniversary)
- **Core thesis:** How a failing textile company became Berkshire; why buying wonderful businesses at fair prices replaced cigar butts; why the conglomerate form and a fortress balance sheet suit permanent ownership.
- **Key frameworks:** three strengths of financial staying power; the ABCs of business decay; CEO selection profile.
- **Sources:** S087.

### Tap Dancing to Work: Warren Buffett on Practically Everything (Carol Loomis, 2012)
- **What it is:** a collection of his Fortune writing and interviews by Carol Loomis, his longtime letter editor (S085). The corpus holds two of his Fortune articles directly: the 1977 inflation article (S102) and the 2012 stocks-versus-gold-and-bonds piece (S103).
- **Core thesis:** inflation is a tax on investors; productive businesses beat currency assets and gold over time.
- **Sources:** S085 (mention), S102, S103.

## Books he teaches from

### The Intelligent Investor (Benjamin Graham, 1949)
- **Core thesis as he teaches it:** three ideas protect an investor from the crowd: treat stocks as pieces of a business, use the market to serve you rather than instruct you (Mr. Market), and always demand a margin of safety. He singles out the chapters on Mr. Market and margin of safety.
- **Sources that teach it:** S002, S008, S009, S011, S019, S022, S060, S063, S065, S066, S086.

### Security Analysis (Benjamin Graham and David Dodd, 1934)
- **Core thesis as he teaches it:** value a security from the underlying business's earning power and assets, not from market sentiment. He credits it as the base of his method and of the Graham-and-Dodd investors he admires.
- **Sources that teach it:** S008, S012, S013, S014, S061.

### Common Stocks and Uncommon Profits (Philip Fisher, 1958)
- **Core thesis as he teaches it:** quality of the business and its management matters; learn it through "scuttlebutt" from customers, suppliers, competitors, and former employees. This pushed him from cheap stocks toward great businesses.
- **Sources that teach it:** S004, S008, S042, S085.

### Poor Charlie's Almanack (Charlie Munger's talks and essays)
- **Core thesis as he presents it:** Munger's worldly wisdom: invert, avoid stupidity, use many mental models, and buy wonderful businesses at fair prices.
- **Sources that mention it:** S019, S020, S077, S078, S079, S080.

### The Outsiders (William Thorndike, 2012)
- **Core thesis as he presents it:** CEOs who excelled at capital allocation (including Tom Murphy, whom Buffett holds up as a model) beat celebrated operators.
- **Sources that mention it:** S085.

### The General Theory (John Maynard Keynes, 1936)
- **How he uses it:** He points students to its chapter on markets, credits Keynes for moving from forecasting business cycles to analyzing individual businesses, and quotes its line that worldly wisdom says it is better for reputation to fail conventionally than to succeed unconventionally, as a warning against herd behavior.
- **Sources that mention it:** S002, S077.
