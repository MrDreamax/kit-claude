# Stories: Jeff Bezos

The stories he reuses, with the lesson he draws. One or two lines each.

1. **Leaving D. E. Shaw (1994).** His boss said the online bookstore was a better idea for someone without a good job and asked him to think for 48 hours; he used the regret test and left. Lesson: on a personal leap, minimize regret, not short-term loss. (S004, S020, S043, S044)

2. **Raising the first million.** About 60 meetings, 22 angels at roughly $50,000 each, the first question always "what's the internet?", and he told backers, including his parents, there was a 70 percent chance they would lose it. Lesson: expect many noes and be honest about the odds. (S001, S014, S030, S041, S043)

3. **Packing tables, not knee pads.** Packing orders on a concrete floor, he proposed knee pads; a colleague said "packing tables", and productivity doubled. Lesson: fix the cause, and take good ideas from anyone. (S003, S043, S044, S047)

4. **"Amazon.toast" (1997).** Barnes & Noble launched online with about 30,000 employees against Amazon's 125; he told staff to fear customers, because competitors would never send them money. Lesson: under threat, double down on the customer. (S011, S037, S041, S047)

5. **$113 to $6.** After the bubble, the stock collapsed in under a year while every internal metric improved, and Amazon already had the cash it needed. Lesson: the stock is not the company; watch fundamentals and keep enough runway. (S008, S023, S041, S044)

6. **Prime from a junior engineer.** An engineer proposed an all-you-can-eat shipping buffet; finance's model was horrifying and the heaviest users came first, but the trend lines were good. Lesson: some bets are judgment calls the math cannot settle. (S044, S061, S062)

7. **Third-party sellers on Amazon's own pages (2000).** Letting sellers compete on the product detail pages caused deep internal fear of cannibalization; third-party units later passed half of sales. Lesson: back the customer-benefit logic when the downside cannot be proven. (S041, S053, S064, S066)

8. **Auctions, then zShops, then Marketplace.** Two failed versions came before the one that worked. Lesson: stay stubborn on the vision and flexible on the form. (S018, S062, S063)

9. **Fire phone to Echo.** Four years of work on a phone no one bought; its people and lessons sped up Echo and Alexa. Lesson: salvage failures into the next bet. (S022, S066)

10. **The brewery's generator and AWS.** A 300-year-old brewery once made its own electricity, and it never made the beer taste better; AWS came from Amazon's own internal pain at scale. Lesson: rent undifferentiated heavy lifting, and productize what you built for yourself. (S001, S012, S028)

11. **Buying The Washington Post (2013).** Two gates: is it important, and is it hopeless? He paid the asking price with no negotiation or due diligence because he trusted Don Graham, moved it to national digital subscriptions, and years later had to cut and re-turn it. Lesson: take on a turnaround only if it matters, and expect fixes to expire. (S001, S003, S018, S030, S033, S044)

12. **"How much do we have?"** Mid-way through Kindle, his CFO asked how much he would spend; that was his answer. Amazon had never built hardware, so it hired hardware engineers instead of shrinking the vision. Lesson: once committed to a conviction bet, do it right. (S028, S055, S056)

13. **Calling customer service in the meeting.** Metrics said waits were under 60 seconds, but customers complained; he dialed the number in the weekly business review and the room waited over ten minutes. Lesson: when anecdotes and data disagree, suspect the metric. (S045)

14. **The table that always comes back.** During customer service training an agent whispered that a caller would return a table that always arrived scratched. Lesson: give the front line an andon cord and fix defects at the source. (S029)

15. **Pets.com and living.com.** Land-rush stakes in single-category startups failed when financing dried up, and he refused to rescue them with Amazon's capital. Lesson: know when a mental model has expired, and do not throw good money after bad. (S008, S027, S048)

16. **"I disagree and commit."** He doubted an Amazon Studios original, said so, and backed the team anyway. Lesson: even the boss disagrees and commits, which beats a long persuasion fight. (S064)

17. **Harder to be kind than clever.** At ten he told his grandmother how many years smoking had cost her; she cried, and his grandfather said it is harder to be kind than clever. Lesson: gifts are easy; choices define you. (S020, S023, S047)

18. **The grandfather's ranch.** Summers fixing a broken bulldozer and doing vet work themselves taught him any problem is solvable. Lesson: resourcefulness is an attitude. (S003, S007, S023, S045)

19. **The tenants and the Christmas tree.** Renters nailed a tree to a hardwood floor. Lesson: owners think long term; tenants do not. (S018, S051)

20. **Profitable too soon.** Amazon was profitable six months after opening in 1995, which he calls a mistake for a business growing that fast. Lesson: when customers love it and growth is explosive, reinvest. (S009)

21. **Too many ideas.** An early executive told him his flow of ideas was overwhelming the company; he began keeping a private list and releasing ideas at the rate the team could absorb. Lesson: meter founder ideas. (S023)
