# Books: Jeff Bezos

He has one book. No book text was used; the entry is built from the public letters and speeches it collects.

## Invent and Wander: The Collected Writings of Jeff Bezos (2020, introduction by Walter Isaacson)

- **What it is:** A collection of his Amazon shareholder letters from 1997 to 2020, plus speeches and interviews, published as he handed over the CEO role.
- **Core thesis (our words):** Invention, driven by customer obsession and long-term thinking, is the root of durable value. A company stays alive by staying in Day 1: experimenting, accepting failure, deciding fast, and refusing to let process and proxies replace judgment.
- **Key frameworks it carries:** Day 1 versus Day 2; Type 1 and Type 2 decisions; disagree and commit; working backwards from customer needs; what's not going to change; the cost, price, and growth flywheel; controllable inputs over outputs; free cash flow per share; math-based versus judgment-based decisions; long-tailed bets and the best place to fail; wandering versus efficiency; the four elements of high standards and the six-page memo; create more than you consume. See `frameworks.md` for each.
- **Sources that teach it:** The letters themselves (S038 to S040, S048 to S068), the Princeton baccalaureate (S020), and the 2021 note to employees (S042), which ends "Remember to wander."

## Books and authors he cites (not his own)

- Benjamin Graham, for the voting machine versus weighing machine line (S003, S048, S060).
- Gerard O'Neill's *The High Frontier*, read in high school, behind his space colonies view (S013, S031).
- Richard Dawkins's *The Blind Watchmaker*, quoted in his last letter on the cost of distinctiveness (S068).
