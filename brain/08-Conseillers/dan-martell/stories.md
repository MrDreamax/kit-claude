# Dan Martell: stories he reuses

Each: what happened, the lesson he draws, and where he tells it.

1. **Prison, rehab, and "hello world."** Foster care, addiction, and a high-speed chase in a stolen car put him in jail as a teenager; a guard named Brian told him he did not belong there, and in rehab he found a Java book and an old 486 and taught himself to code. Lesson: one person's belief and a new obsession can redirect a life; the drive that helped early has to be unlearned to avoid chaos at scale. (S006, S011, S013, S014, S017, S018, S019, S069, S106, S107)

2. **Burnout at Spheric.** From about 24 to 28 he did everything himself, worked 100-hour weeks, and got anxiety attacks, adrenal fatigue, and shingles; once he changed how he ran companies, they tripled and the work felt easy. Lesson: founder-as-doer breaks both the founder and the business. (S036, S039, S096, S122)

3. **The lonely Monday after the exit.** He sold his first successful company in his twenties after years of sacrifice, his engagement had ended, and the Monday after the wire hit he felt empty and later had a panic attack. Lesson: success bought with health and relationships is hollow, which is why he built the buyback system. (S012, S013, S015)

4. **Branson's assistant Helen.** At Richard Branson's home he watched Helen route everything inbound and bring only the exceptions to a morning meeting, after which Branson went skiing while running hundreds of companies. Lesson: hand your inbox and calendar to someone who owns them; this pushed him to hire an in-person EA. (S017, S019, S022, S031, S032, S037, S038, S046, S049)

5. **His brother's CC'd assistant.** His brother, running a large real estate business, hired an assistant but only copied them on emails and saw no benefit after months. Lesson: partial delegation gives no leverage; the assistant must own 100% of the inbox. (S020, S049, S054)

6. **Michael the sword seller.** After his wife asked why "the buy back your time guy" was on 8 p.m. sales calls, he hired a man who had sold swords online, gave him call recordings, and Michael closed on his first call and later beat Dan's numbers. Lesson: a recorded process lets someone else sell for you, often better. (S006, S017, S038)

7. **Adam and the 1-3-1.** His head of people had to hire 11 or 12 people in a quarter and asked how; Dan asked for his 1-3-1 and Adam came back the next morning with a plan. Lesson: stop answering; make leaders bring a recommendation. (S020, S031, S036, S038, S122)

8. **Sunday mail and Lisa.** At 26 he spent Sundays sorting mail until he hired Lisa, recorded himself doing the work so she could learn it, and used the hours for lead generation. Lesson: the origin of the camcorder method and the first rung of the ladder. (S006, S046, S097)

9. **Paul Meyer and the lawn.** A billionaire mentor told him a $1M goal means about $500 an hour, so two hours of mowing cost him roughly $960. Lesson: opportunity cost makes cheap tasks expensive. (S019)

10. **Seth Godin and the test project.** While reviewing Clarity, Godin said he cannot work with people until he works with them; Dan built paid test projects into every hire. Lesson: simulate the work before the offer. (S037, S074)

11. **The Procter and Gamble project.** He hired a senior person for a P&G co-funded project, gave full control, and did not monitor; nine months later it had cost about $500K and nearly lost the customer. Lesson: delegate, do not abdicate. (S103; a similar slow-to-fire genius hire in S040)

12. **The $15,000 culture reset.** He offered $15K to anyone not on board with a new direction; he says 36 took it (people or percent is unclear), including some of his best people, and his COO quit weeks later because he had not told leadership first. Lesson: reset culture, but enroll leadership before announcing; do not copy Zappos blindly. (S011, S115)

13. **Ethan's laundry.** His co-founder could not come in to fix a crisis because he had to do laundry, with six wash-and-fold shops on the way. Lesson: founders who underpay themselves cannot buy back the time the business needs. (S005, S108)

14. **The $3M agency that cut to one service.** An offer audit showed 80% of profit came from paid ads for SaaS companies; they cut everything else and doubled to $6M in 18 months with fewer clients. Lesson: simple scales; find the one profitable offer. (S036, S122)

15. **$27M with no delivery.** A friend scaled to $27M on marketing and sales with no fulfillment system, customers complained, and he rebuilt at about half the revenue. Lesson: growth without delivery systems is a house of cards. (S036, S122)

16. **Equity without vesting.** At Flowtown he gave 1% to a business development hire who stopped contributing and had to fly out to get it back before the first round; a portfolio company paid about $600K to buy out an unvested 50/50 co-founder. Lesson: always vest, including founders. (S082)

17. **A dozen direct reports at Flowtown.** After raising venture money he had twelve people reporting to him on top of his own workload. Lesson: cap direct reports at seven and manage through functional metrics. (S088)

18. **Sam's Porsche GT4.** His creative director kept a white Porsche GT4 as his phone wallpaper for years; Dan and his wife bought it for him. Lesson: know your people's dreams and work to make them real; it builds loyalty. (S025, S042, S108, S115)

19. **The client with 35 free hours.** A client who hired an EA called to say his week was empty and asked what to do; Dan told him he forgot to invest and produce. Lesson: buying back time without a Fill plan leaves you empty. (S037, S052, S061)

20. **Tom's two CEOs.** A client running a $12M recruiting firm and a $5M software company hired two CEOs through a recruiter at Dan's urging, and the business doubled in 12 months. Lesson: once you cap out, hire above yourself. (S109)
