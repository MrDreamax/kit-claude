# Stories: Gino Wickman

18 stories he retells. One or two lines each, with the lesson he draws and the source ids. Details vary slightly between tellings (ages, headcounts); the ids show where each version lives.

1. **Turning around his father's business.** In his mid-20s he took over his father's sales-training company, which was in debt and badly run, while his father stayed the visionary. He turned it around, ran it about seven years, and sold it. Lesson: the Visionary and Integrator split, and the seed of EOS. (S027, S029, S034, S042, S063, S077)

2. **The room at the Troy Marriott.** About a year and a half into the turnaround he locked his father and a partner in a hotel room, made his case, and drew the first Accountability Chart with his dad in the visionary seat. Lesson: structure first, and say the hard thing. (S035, S038)

3. **Flying blind, then the Scorecard.** He ran the family business without numbers for about a year and a half until mentor Sam Cupp taught him to watch a handful of weekly numbers. Lesson: a weekly pulse beats gut feel. (S055, S077)

4. **Finding the 90-day pulse.** In the same turnaround he saw leadership alignment fade within about 90 days, so he started meeting quarterly to reset priorities. Lesson: Rocks and the 90-day world. (S025, S035)

5. **Pulling levers.** For about five years and hundreds of client sessions he prescribed tools from books, watched whether the client smiled, and kept only what worked; roughly 200 tools became 20. Lesson: build the system from real client problems, not theory. (S027, S041, S082)

6. **136 issues, six root causes.** Listening to entrepreneurs for a decade, he sorted their 136 recurring complaints into six root causes, which became the Six Key Components. Lesson: treat symptoms at the root. (S026, S043)

7. **Broke at 32.** He hit millionaire at 31, then lost it in the dot-com crash and bad investments and ended up about $200,000 in debt with a young family. He told his wife he needed $15,000 a month by June 30 or he would get a real job. Lesson: keep the money, stay conservative, and set a hard deadline. (S025, S027, S055, S068)

8. **Eighteen months with Don Tinney and no revenue.** The early EOS Implementer model made no money for about a year and a half; during a Starbucks clarity break he switched to an open, abundance-based licensing model and it took off. Lesson: when the model is not working, change the model, and take clarity breaks. (S027, S028)

9. **Thirty-one publishers said no to Traction.** Publishers rejected the book and asked him to drop the narrow target market; he kept it and self-published. Lesson: hold your niche against expert advice. (S032, S082)

10. **The firehose of ideas.** As EOS gained traction, people pushed him to add services, buy a sales-training firm, and broaden the tools. He said no every time and describes it this way: "imagine the waves crashing against a rock." (S030) Lesson: Core Focus turns noise into a no. (S030, S092)

11. **The 2008 to 2009 recession.** EOS took roughly a 30% revenue hit right after launch. This time he was prepared, told people the brutal facts, cut spending, and worked through it. Lesson: the two bad years are coming; prepare in the good ones. (S038, S068)

12. **The Honey Badgers.** Fifteen of the best Implementers threatened a mass exodus. He and Don decided they could let them go and confronted it; the group turned around and one later led EOS Worldwide. Lesson: confront issues even with your best people. (S030, S038)

13. **The Magnificent Seven.** A marketing firm interviewed his seven best clients and all seven named a better quality of life, not business results, as the top benefit. He shrugged it off for years until Don Tinney named it The EOS Life. Lesson: listen to what clients actually value. (S025, S028, S029)

14. **"Are you going home?"** When he was about 28, his four-year-old daughter asked if he was going home, because she rarely saw him there. It started his habit of taking every August off. Lesson: workaholism costs the people you love, and time off makes you better. (S025, S028, S029)

15. **Selling EOS Worldwide.** He had built it to about 200 people, past his sweet spot, so he sold it. He told every employee months before closing, against expert advice, let about 30 invest, defined an ideal buyer, and kept 12.5%. Lesson: sell well, openly, with a runway, and only if your culture can take the truth. (S030, S036, S038, S041, S075)

16. **Replacing himself as Visionary.** He made a five-year plan, defined what the next Visionary had to look like (different from himself), narrowed candidates over 6 to 12 months, coached and shadowed his successor for about a year, and handed over the gavel in pieces. Lesson: succession is a deliberate, multi-year project. (S036, S038)

17. **Self-publishing, then giving it up.** He self-published for about three years, hated the distribution work, and moved to a publisher even though it cost him two to three times the revenue and profit. Lesson: pay to delegate what drains you. (S051, S078)

18. **The pandemic plan.** In the first days of COVID he built a four-hour worst-case financial plan, then spent weeks talking to clients and the market, where he got his best ideas. Lesson: prepare, then get closer to customers than ever. (S056, S082, S098)
